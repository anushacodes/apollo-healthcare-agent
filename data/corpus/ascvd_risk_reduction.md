# ASCVD Risk Assessment and Reduction

## Overview
Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) risk assessment estimates a
patient's probability of a cardiovascular event (myocardial infarction or
stroke) over a defined time horizon, most commonly the next 10 years, to guide
the intensity of preventive therapy.

## Risk Estimation
Pooled cohort equations combine age, sex, race, total cholesterol, HDL
cholesterol, systolic blood pressure, treatment status for hypertension,
diabetes status, and smoking status to produce a 10-year ASCVD risk estimate.
Patients are commonly categorized as:
- Low risk: below 5%.
- Borderline risk: 5% to below 7.5%.
- Intermediate risk: 7.5% to below 20%.
- High risk: 20% or above.
Risk-enhancing factors (family history of premature ASCVD, chronic
inflammatory disease such as SLE or rheumatoid arthritis, chronic kidney
disease, elevated lipoprotein(a)) can reclassify borderline or intermediate
risk patients toward more intensive therapy.

## Statin Therapy
- High-intensity statin therapy is recommended for patients with clinical
  ASCVD, LDL-C ≥ 190 mg/dL, or diabetes with additional risk factors.
- Moderate-to-high-intensity statin therapy is generally recommended for
  intermediate-risk patients after a risk discussion, with intensity guided
  by risk-enhancing factors.
- For borderline-risk patients, risk-enhancing factors or coronary artery
  calcium scoring can help decide whether to initiate statin therapy.

## Additional Risk-Reduction Measures
- Blood pressure control to guideline targets substantially reduces ASCVD
  risk independent of lipid management.
- Smoking cessation produces a rapid and substantial reduction in
  cardiovascular risk.
- Antiplatelet therapy (aspirin) is generally reserved for secondary
  prevention (established ASCVD) rather than routine primary prevention,
  given bleeding risk that often offsets benefit in low-risk primary
  prevention populations.
- Optimal management of diabetes, particularly with agents demonstrating
  cardiovascular benefit (SGLT2 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists), further
  reduces cardiovascular risk in patients with type 2 diabetes.

## Chronic Inflammatory Disease and ASCVD Risk
Patients with chronic inflammatory conditions such as SLE, rheumatoid
arthritis, or psoriatic arthritis carry cardiovascular risk substantially
higher than predicted by traditional risk calculators alone, likely driven by
chronic vascular inflammation, corticosteroid exposure, and antiphospholipid
antibodies where present. Clinicians should treat calculated risk as a
conservative floor rather than a precise estimate in this population and have
a lower threshold for statin initiation and aggressive risk-factor
modification.
