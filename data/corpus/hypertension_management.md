# Hypertension Management

## Overview
Hypertension is sustained elevation of systemic arterial blood pressure and is
one of the most important modifiable risk factors for cardiovascular disease,
stroke, heart failure, and chronic kidney disease. Most cases are primary
(essential) hypertension without a single identifiable cause; secondary causes
should be considered in resistant, early-onset, or abruptly worsening disease.

## Diagnostic Thresholds
Blood pressure is generally classified as:
- Normal: below 120/80 mmHg.
- Elevated: systolic 120-129 with diastolic below 80.
- Stage 1 hypertension: systolic 130-139 or diastolic 80-89.
- Stage 2 hypertension: systolic ≥ 140 or diastolic ≥ 90.
Diagnosis should be confirmed with repeated measurements, ideally including
out-of-office readings (home or ambulatory monitoring), to avoid overdiagnosis
from white-coat effect.

## Lifestyle Interventions
Weight loss, the DASH dietary pattern, sodium restriction, regular aerobic
exercise, and limiting alcohol intake each produce measurable blood pressure
reductions and are recommended as first-line or adjunctive therapy for all
patients regardless of pharmacologic treatment.

## First-Line Pharmacotherapy
Four drug classes are considered first-line, with the choice individualized to
comorbidities:
- Thiazide or thiazide-like diuretics.
- ACE inhibitors.
- Angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs).
- Calcium channel blockers.
ACE inhibitors and ARBs are preferred in patients with diabetes, chronic
kidney disease with proteinuria, or heart failure, given renal- and
cardio-protective effects beyond blood pressure lowering alone. Beta-blockers
are not typically first-line for uncomplicated hypertension but are indicated
when a compelling comorbid indication exists, such as coexisting heart
failure or post-myocardial infarction.

## Treatment Targets
A target of below 130/80 mmHg is reasonable for most adults with
hypertension, particularly those with additional cardiovascular risk factors,
though individualization is appropriate in frail or elderly patients where
more conservative targets may reduce orthostatic and fall risk.

## Resistant Hypertension
Blood pressure remaining above target despite three antihypertensive agents
of different classes, including a diuretic, at optimized doses defines
resistant hypertension and warrants evaluation for secondary causes
(renovascular disease, primary aldosteronism, obstructive sleep apnea) and
consideration of a fourth agent such as spironolactone.
