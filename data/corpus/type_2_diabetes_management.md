# Type 2 Diabetes Management

## Overview
Type 2 diabetes mellitus is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by
insulin resistance and progressive beta-cell dysfunction, leading to
hyperglycemia. It is a major risk factor for cardiovascular disease,
nephropathy, retinopathy, and neuropathy, so management addresses both
glycemic control and cardiovascular/renal risk reduction.

## Diagnostic Thresholds
- Fasting plasma glucose ≥ 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L).
- HbA1c ≥ 6.5%.
- Random plasma glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) with classic symptoms.
- 2-hour plasma glucose ≥ 200 mg/dL during an oral glucose tolerance test.

## Glycemic Targets
A general HbA1c target of below 7% is reasonable for most non-pregnant
adults, individualized based on age, comorbidity burden, hypoglycemia risk,
and life expectancy — looser targets (e.g., below 8%) are appropriate for
frail or older patients with significant comorbidity.

## First-Line and Add-On Therapy
- Metformin remains first-line therapy for most patients due to its efficacy,
  safety profile, weight neutrality, and low cost, assuming no
  contraindication (eGFR below 30 mL/min, acute illness with risk of
  hypoperfusion).
- In patients with established cardiovascular disease, heart failure, or
  chronic kidney disease, an SGLT2 inhibitor or GLP-1 receptor agonist is
  recommended independent of HbA1c, given demonstrated cardiovascular and
  renal benefits beyond glucose lowering.
- Additional agents (sulfonylureas, DPP-4 inhibitors, thiazolidinediones,
  insulin) are added based on individualized targets, side-effect profile,
  and cost considerations.

## Metformin Precautions
Metformin should be temporarily held around the time of iodinated contrast
administration in patients with reduced renal function, during acute
illness with risk of dehydration or hypoxia, and during hospital admissions
where renal perfusion may be unstable, due to the risk of lactic acidosis. It
is generally restarted once renal function and clinical status are confirmed
stable.

## Monitoring and Complication Screening
Routine care includes HbA1c every 3-6 months, annual dilated eye exam,
annual urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio and eGFR, foot examination, and lipid
panel, alongside blood pressure control given the compounding cardiovascular
risk of diabetes plus hypertension.

## Hypoglycemia Awareness
Patients on insulin or sulfonylureas are at risk of hypoglycemia; treatment
regimens should be adjusted to avoid frequent low blood glucose episodes,
particularly in older adults, since recurrent hypoglycemia is associated with
increased cardiovascular events and impaired hypoglycemia awareness over
time.
