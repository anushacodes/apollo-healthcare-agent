# Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction (HFrEF)

## Overview
Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction is defined by a left ventricular
ejection fraction (LVEF) of 40% or less accompanied by signs and symptoms of
heart failure such as dyspnea, fatigue, and fluid retention. It results from
impaired systolic contractile function and can be caused by ischemic heart
disease, longstanding hypertension, valvular disease, or non-ischemic
cardiomyopathies.

## Diagnosis
Diagnosis combines clinical assessment (exertional dyspnea, orthopnea,
paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, peripheral oedema), elevated natriuretic
peptides (BNP or NT-proBNP), and echocardiography confirming reduced LVEF.
Chest radiography may show cardiomegaly or pulmonary vascular congestion.

## Guideline-Directed Medical Therapy (GDMT)
Four pillars of therapy are recommended for essentially all HFrEF patients
unless contraindicated, ideally initiated and up-titrated in parallel rather
than sequentially:
- ACE inhibitor, ARB, or angiotensin receptor-neprilysin inhibitor (ARNI,
  e.g., sacubitril-valsartan) — ARNI is preferred over ACE inhibitor/ARB when
  tolerated, given mortality benefit demonstrated over enalapril.
- Evidence-based beta-blocker (carvedilol, bisoprolol, or sustained-release
  metoprolol succinate) — started at low dose and up-titrated as tolerated.
- Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (spironolactone or eplerenone) —
  requires monitoring of potassium and renal function.
- SGLT2 inhibitor (dapagliflozin or empagliflozin) — beneficial regardless of
  diabetes status and now considered a foundational therapy alongside the
  other three pillars.

## Diuretic Management
Loop diuretics (e.g., furosemide) are used for symptomatic relief of
congestion but do not have a proven mortality benefit; dosing is titrated to
euvolemia based on daily weights, symptoms, and renal function, with careful
attention to electrolyte disturbances during aggressive diuresis.

## Monitoring
Renal function and potassium should be checked after initiation or
up-titration of ACE inhibitor/ARB/ARNI and mineralocorticoid receptor
antagonists. Repeat echocardiography is used to track LVEF recovery over time
and to reassess candidacy for device therapy (ICD or CRT) if LVEF remains
severely reduced despite optimized GDMT for at least three months.

## Comorbidity Considerations
Renal impairment is common in decompensated heart failure and can limit
diuretic response and RAAS-inhibitor dosing (cardiorenal syndrome).
Metformin is typically held during acute decompensation and renal
impairment due to lactic acidosis risk, and restarted once renal function and
hemodynamic status stabilize.
