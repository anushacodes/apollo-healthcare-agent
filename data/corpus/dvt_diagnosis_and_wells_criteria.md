# Deep Vein Thrombosis: Diagnosis and the Wells Criteria

## Overview
Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) is thrombus formation within the deep venous
system, most commonly the lower extremities. It shares risk factors and
pathophysiology with pulmonary embolism, since a proximal DVT can embolize to
the pulmonary circulation.

## Risk Factors
Classic contributors follow Virchow's triad — venous stasis, endothelial
injury, and hypercoagulability — and include recent surgery or prolonged
immobilization, active malignancy, prior VTE, pregnancy and the postpartum
period, estrogen-containing contraception or hormone therapy, obesity, and
inherited or acquired thrombophilia such as antiphospholipid syndrome, Factor
V Leiden, or protein C/S deficiency.

## The Wells Score for DVT
The Wells clinical prediction rule assigns points for the following features
to stratify pretest probability:
- Active cancer (treatment within 6 months or palliative).
- Paralysis, paresis, or recent plaster immobilization of the lower
  extremity.
- Recently bedridden for 3+ days or major surgery within 12 weeks.
- Localized tenderness along the deep venous system.
- Entire leg swollen.
- Calf swelling at least 3 cm greater than the asymptomatic side.
- Pitting oedema confined to the symptomatic leg.
- Collateral superficial veins (non-varicose).
- Previously documented DVT.
- An alternative diagnosis at least as likely as DVT (subtracts points).
A higher score indicates higher pretest probability, guiding whether D-dimer
testing alone is sufficient to exclude DVT or whether compression ultrasound
should be obtained directly.

## Diagnostic Approach
- Low pretest probability with a negative D-dimer effectively excludes DVT
  without further imaging.
- Moderate-to-high pretest probability, or a positive D-dimer, warrants
  compression ultrasonography of the deep venous system, which is the primary
  imaging modality due to its accessibility and high sensitivity for proximal
  DVT.
- Serial ultrasound may be used when an initial study is negative but
  clinical suspicion remains high, to detect thrombus propagation from the
  calf veins.

## Treatment Overview
Anticoagulation is the mainstay of treatment, generally for a minimum of
three months, following the same general principles and duration
considerations as management of pulmonary embolism (see dedicated pulmonary
embolism document), since DVT and PE are considered part of the same venous
thromboembolic disease spectrum.
